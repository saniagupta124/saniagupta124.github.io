Dysevolution

A 2-D top-down puzzle game

Project Overview

Dysevolution is a 2D top-down puzzle game where you evolve through different time periods and occupations. You must complete tasks on time to keep health up and survive. As time passes, you take new roles fitting the era, including hunter-gatherer, farmer, and office worker.

This game highlights evolutionary mismatch-where humans are placed in conditions not corresponding to those they evolved for-by having players upgrade skills and "evolve" to succeed using a skill tree. In later levels, the conditions for winning, and the player can't succeed as adopted skills fail to meet modern life's demands.

C# & Unity Game Design Version Control

My Contribution

My teammate and I collaboratively planned the game structure and code architecture, bouncing ideas off one another and refining them throughout development. I co-led the design and implementation in Unity using C#, creating systems like the hunting and agriculture mechanics as well as the final office minigame. I also built core features such as the health bar, timer, and grid-based planting. In the final stretch, I debugged scene sequencing issues and ensured the skill tree state persisted across levels for a smooth, cohesive experience.

Moon Patrol

Recreation of the classic 2-D runner

Project Overview

Moon Patrol is a landmark 2D side-scrolling runner where players control a moon buggy navigating craters, mines, and enemy attacks through increasingly difficult checkpoints. Originally released in 1982, it introduced parallax scrolling and dynamic difficulty that shaped future games.

For this project, my partner and I analyzed the game's animation, sound, mechanics, and level design to understand what made it memorable. This study helped us honor its feel while rebuilding it from the ground up in Unity. The process sharpened our skills in recreating gameplay systems, timing, and player feedback, core to crafting engaging experiences.

C# & Unity Project Management Version Control

My Contribution

For this project, I contributed to core gameplay features including checkpoint implementation, enemy behavior, and UI elements such as the menu and scoring screens. I developed systems to manage checkpoint transitions and spawn labeled checkpoint objects for easier navigation and grading. Additionally, I programmed enemy attack patterns like the UFO dive and refined gameplay pacing through speed adjustments and animations. Throughout development, I collaborated closely with my partner to integrate art assets, sound, and animations, ensuring a cohesive and polished final product.

DV Prevention Animation

Animated short for Narika's teen program

Project Overview

This video is a 3D animated sequence for Narika's Teen Dating Violence curriculum, part of the S.A.P.H.A.L. program. Using Autodesk Maya, I modeled, animated, and rendered characters and environments to vividly depict a challenging scenario faced by the protagonist, Sakina. The animation explores complex emotional and social dynamics, aiming to raise awareness and foster discussions around healthy relationships and personal boundaries among teens.

Autodesk Maya 3D Animation Narrative Design

My Contribution

I independently managed the entire animation pipeline-from storyboarding, character modeling, rigging, UV mapping, weight painting, and texturing, to animating, lighting, rendering, and camera animation. I then edited the final film and integrated a voiceover narration to effectively convey the scenario. Throughout, I ensured the animation aligned closely with the educational goals of the curriculum.

Barnyard Bonfire

A tense multiplayer stacking game

Project Overview

Barnyard Bonfire is a fast-paced, physically interactive multiplayer game developed by four USC students. Players are farm animals who must cooperate to escape a burning barn represented by an Arduino fire alarm that lights and buzzes as time runs out. Using irregular objects, they stack a tower to reach a window while following "forbidden rules" tied to their roles. For example, the chicken uses chopsticks to place pieces, mimicking a beak.

The game was named Most Innovative Game Design and was a USC Games Expo finalist.

Game Design Project Management Narrative Design

My Contribution

I contributed to the game's design by helping develop the core mechanics, forbidden rule system, and tower components. As the primary artist, I illustrated the character cards and designed visual assets for the game. I also played a central role in project management, coordinating deadlines and organizing team deliverables throughout development.

Gem Rush

Immersive VR endless runner

Project Overview

Gem Rush is a VR game developed for Carnegie Mellon University's National High School Game Academy, set in a cave automatic virtual environment (CAVE). Players ride a cart through tunnels, collecting gems, dodging obstacles, and avoiding bats, spiders, and a miner's spirit. The setup uses three projector screens and a hydraulic platform, transporting the player to the mine.

The gameplay challenges players to balance reflexes and timing, with separate controls for gem collection and enemy attacks. Gem Rush blends fast-paced action with immersive VR to deliver a thrilling experience.

C# & Unity Autodesk Maya Project Management

My Contribution

As the 3D artist and producer, I created all 3D assets, including the environment, mine cart, enemies, and collectibles. In my role as producer, I managed the project timeline, created daily task sheets, and led team check-ins to ensure clear communication and timely progress. I facilitated collaboration between designers and programmers, keeping development aligned and on track. I also contributed to programming tasks, including procedural generation of cave segments and player interaction mechanics, supporting a seamless VR experience under a tight schedule.

Somebody's Watching

Immersive VR endless runner

Project Overview

Somebody's Watching is a 3D animated project exploring liminal spaces-abandoned, transitional environments that evoke unease. Through interconnected scenes of forest, neighborhood, bedroom, and graveyard, this work invites viewers to experience a surreal, uncanny world where being watched amplifies anxiety.

Using muted yellows and purples and seamless looped animations, the project blends tangible and artificial elements to immerse viewers. Displayed in sequence, the scenes encourage viewers to question their fear and reflect on isolation and the uncanny.

Autodesk Maya 3D Animation Narrative Design

My Contribution

I independently managed the entire project, including storyboarding, 3D modeling, texturing, rigging, animating, and lighting each scene. I created seamless looped animations for continuous projection and edited the final video installation. Additionally, I conducted research on the concept of the uncanny and its emotional impact to inform the atmosphere and design of the work. I also coordinated the projection setup for the exhibit at The Harker School.
